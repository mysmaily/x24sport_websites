# Quality gates

Use this rubric for every proof and final image. A single hard failure rejects the image.

## 1. Design fidelity — hard gate

- Match the garment silhouette, collar, sleeve length, cuffs, panels, and hem.
- Match every major color and boundary; reject hue drift that changes the product.
- Match artwork/logo location, orientation, relative scale, and count.
- Keep important readable text correctly spelled. Reject mirrored, scrambled, duplicated, or invented text.
- Keep the correct front/back design on each visible side.
- Keep one design consistent across all wearers unless the brief assigns variants.
- Preserve approved customer/team artwork that is part of the exact garment design. Reject source-vendor marks, photographer watermarks, URLs, corner logos, or unrequested brand additions.
- Require practical sleeves on every shirt. Reject tank tops, sleeveless jerseys, cap sleeves exposing the armpit, or oversized armholes. For sleeveless inputs, accept only a faithful short-sleeve conversion.

## 2. People and age — hard gate

- Require distinct, attractive, believable Vietnamese/Southeast Asian faces rather than cloned identities.
- Require natural skin texture, pores, fine lines, asymmetry, hair strands, moist but not glassy eyes, and plausible teeth.
- Reject waxy skin, porcelain blur, face-filter smoothness, doll eyes, melted ears, repeated facial features, or uncanny smiles.
- Inspect every hand, finger, arm, leg, foot, and joint. Reject missing, fused, extra, twisted, or implausibly occluded anatomy.
- Require plausible scale, perspective, gaze, physical contact, balance, and weight.
- Match the requested age group. Never substitute children for adults or adults for children.
- For children, require age-appropriate proportions, clothing, pose, activity, makeup, and framing.

## 3. Garment realism — hard gate

- Require recognizable cotton jersey or performance-knit texture appropriate to the design.
- Require seams, collar rib, sleeve seam, hem stitching, thickness, gravity, and natural wrinkles.
- Make the print follow folds and body curvature without appearing pasted on or melting into the fabric.
- Reject plastic shine, rubber surfaces, metallic cloth unless specified, flat vector fabric, excessive edge sharpness, or painted-on shadows.
- Keep fit plausible across different bodies without changing the product cut.

## 4. Scene and group realism — hard gate

- Require one coherent light direction, color temperature, shadow softness, and contact shadow.
- Ground every person on the same plane; reject floating feet, intersecting bodies, impossible overlaps, and mismatched scale.
- Use varied but coordinated expressions and poses; reject cloned poses and everyone staring identically at camera.
- Keep props secondary, anatomically usable, and free of malformed packaging or random readable brands.
- Require real photographic depth, lens perspective, and background detail; reject synthetic blur halos or cutout edges.

## 5. Commerce and composition — hard gate

- Show the design clearly on enough people to sell the uniform.
- Keep faces and garments away from unsafe crop edges and website text-safe zones when requested.
- Avoid wardrobe or props that compete with shirt colors and artwork.
- Reject unintended captions, random typography, watermarks, signatures, logos, or borders.
- Match the requested aspect ratio and visual role.
- Require 3–7 models and verify the set varies count and formation instead of repeating a fixed template.
- Require exactly one bundled Mayaodongphuc logo, undistorted and fully visible in a safe corner without covering a face, hair, hand, garment, or important action.
- Reject any Mayaodongphuc corner logo printed onto the garment itself.
- Require deliberate graphic design: clear hierarchy, aligned grid, consistent icon family, balanced margins, branded navy/orange palette, and a compact footprint that leaves the photography dominant.
- Reject any hotline, feature list, badge rail, promotional copy, floating label, or text box added after the logo.
- Require bundled logo pixels to remain crisp, undistorted, correctly spelled, and identical across a set. Reject AI-redrawn versions or generated approximations of the logo assets.

## Acceptance score

After all hard gates pass, score each dimension from 0–2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design | wrong | minor noncritical drift | faithful |
| Faces/anatomy | obvious AI defect | slightly stiff | natural |
| Fabric/print | plastic or pasted | plausible | tactile and integrated |
| Scene/group | incoherent | usable | candid and coherent |
| Commerce | weak product view | usable | strong catalog image |

Accept at 9/10 or 10/10 only. A score never overrides a hard-gate failure.

## Targeted corrections

Correct only the failing dimension in an iteration:

- Design: “Keep the accepted people, pose, setting, lighting, and crop. Correct only the shirt to match [specific collar/panel/artwork detail].”
- Face: “Keep the accepted garment and composition. Replace the waxy facial treatment with natural skin texture, facial asymmetry, lively eyes, and plausible teeth.”
- Hands: “Keep everything else. Repair only [person/location] hand to five anatomically correct fingers with natural grip and occlusion.”
- Fabric: “Keep the design unchanged. Add subtle [cotton/performance-knit] weave, seam thickness, hem stitching, gravity wrinkles, and print deformation following the cloth.”
- Consistency: “Keep the accepted design and cast. Make all shirts use the identical approved color blocking and artwork placement.”

Inspect the corrected image again from gate 1; edits can regress previously accepted details.
