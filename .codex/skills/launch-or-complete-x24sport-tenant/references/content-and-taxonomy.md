# Content and Taxonomy Reference

## Build the information model from buyer decisions

Start from what buyers need to select, compare, trust, customize, and order.
Separate these layers:

- **Category:** a durable product family that deserves navigation and a landing
  page.
- **Attribute/filter:** a cross-cutting distinction used to narrow results.
- **Variant:** a purchasable/configurable form of one product.
- **Audience/use case:** a curated landing only when it has distinct intent and
  enough useful content/products.
- **Guide/post:** educational or inspirational content, not a disguised thin
  category page.

Avoid categories created only for every keyword combination. Keep filter URLs
non-indexable unless a specific curated landing is intentionally approved.

## Taxonomy proposal

Provide a tree with, for every node:

- customer-facing name and stable ASCII slug;
- parent, scope, and inclusion/exclusion rule;
- distinguishing attributes and recommended filters;
- expected product set or factual data source;
- landing-page purpose and indexability;
- internal links to relevant guides, services, or conversion paths;
- migration mapping from existing categories when applicable.

Identify collisions, duplicate concepts, orphan products, empty categories, and
hard-to-reverse slug decisions before implementation.

## Content inventory

Inventory every required page or record with:

- page type and URL;
- primary audience task and conversion goal;
- factual owner/source;
- current state: keep, rewrite, create, migrate, merge, redirect, or retire;
- required media and alt-text intent;
- title, H1, summary/meta purpose, body sections, CTA, and related links;
- publication state and unresolved factual fields.

Typical scope to evaluate—not blindly create—includes homepage, catalog,
category pages, product/service details, customization/process, size/fabric or
buying guides, pricing/quote guidance, about/trust, contact, FAQs, policies,
search, and useful editorial content.

## Product-content completeness

Before publishing a product, verify factual name, slug, stable identity/SKU,
category, price or contact-price state, availability, variants/attributes,
short and detailed descriptions, customization facts, gallery ownership, useful
alt text, metadata, canonical behavior, and structured-data consistency.

Do not hide missing facts in polished prose. Keep incomplete records as drafts
and add each missing fact to the input ledger.

## Content quality

- Write for a shopper's next decision; remove CMS, AI, SEO, route, and QA talk.
- Use concrete verified details instead of generic superlatives.
- Give category introductions enough meaning to orient and choose, without
  pushing the first product row below the initial mobile viewport.
- Keep voice, terminology, size conventions, price states, and contact actions
  consistent across visible content, metadata, JSON-LD, and CMS.
- Link related categories, products, guides, and conversion pages intentionally.
- Do not publish claims copied from competitors or sibling tenants without
  explicit authorization and factual validation.

## Content implementation gate

Before applying a batch, show the proposed create/update/merge/redirect plan and
dry-run results. After applying, verify tenant ownership, relationships, media,
rendering, navigation reachability, sitemap eligibility, and sibling isolation.
